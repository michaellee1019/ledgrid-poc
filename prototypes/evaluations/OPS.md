# Event-operations and maintenance usability evaluation

## Persona and method

I evaluated these concepts as the operator who has to run the installation during an event and maintain it when something looks wrong. I know the software and hardware vocabulary, but I am usually making decisions with a room waiting on me. My priorities are: establish the physical truth quickly, make consequential commands hard to misread, preserve provider-qualified identity, and distinguish limited evidence from failure or health.

I used every candidate in the browser before reading its documentation: first at 1440 × 1000, then at 390 × 844. At each size I began from a fresh load and followed the controls that looked most likely to complete the shared adventures. I then read each candidate's README and decision document and compared the demonstrated behavior with `web/app.py`, `ipc/scene_contract.py`, and the plant/presentation contracts. Fixture data was evaluated as representative interaction state, not penalized merely for lacking a live connection. However, fixture claims were penalized when they presented simulated or internally contradictory state as authoritative hardware evidence.

Task result is **success**, **partial**, or **failure**. Confidence is the operator's confidence that the resulting physical state is the intended one, from 1 (guessing) to 5 (independently clear). The weighted total is the sum of `(category score / 5) × category weight`.

## Verdict

Wall Conductor is the strongest operating base, narrowly ahead of Room Tune. It combines a persistent physical-live statement, excellent catalog identity, unusually good evidence detail, and a broad set of maintainer surfaces. Room Tune is the most candid about the limits of receiver evidence and is the cleanest phone experience, but it offers no usable recovery after simulated scene drift. Lumen Loom has the best deliberate scene-performance threshold and an effective three-pane mental model, but its phone emergency controls and evidence freshness are weak. Finder Wall Studio looks closest to a traditional expert tool, yet a build-only entry can cross its supposedly disabled safety boundary and its receiver inspector contradicts the live state. Lumen Path is excellent for guided exploration, but stale old-state confirmations and a healthy report while the wall is stopped and powered off make it unsuitable as the operational source of truth.

## Existing backend contract: tournament-wide implications

The current backend supports more of these concepts than a visual prototype might suggest, but it also establishes several non-negotiable boundaries:

- `/api/v1/components` can supply a unified catalog with provider, role, readiness, and scene compatibility. Provider-qualified identity must stay intact. The component-preset detail route is not by itself collision-safe: a shared component ID can return 409 without a provider discriminator.
- `/api/v1/scene/preview` already labels receiver-native rendering as host simulation and says `live_state_mutated: false`. Candidates should reuse those exact truth semantics.
- A scene is one background plus at most the fixed clock overlay. Its accepted clip policy is `clip_to_wall`; stale policy is `hold` or `clear_after_lease`. UI-only wrap/reject/stop-scene choices should not survive to Apply.
- Receiver-native scene background execution is much narrower than catalog visibility: the current path permits `compiled_rainbow` only when both relevant rollout flags are enabled. Catalog visibility is not execution readiness.
- Scene preset saving correctly rejects Vibe, plant, and output state. Candidate layout-only wording should be preserved.
- `/api/v1/receivers/status/refresh` returns HTTP 202 with request/command identity. **Accepted** is not **fresh**, **agreed**, or **healthy**. A client needs to poll status, verify a newer observation/timestamp tied to the request, and then state what was observed.
- Device state directly covers power, brightness, animation, and preset. Vibe, plant modes, FPS/speed, and interaction are separate calls or contracts. A candidate presenting one atomic global Apply must handle partial failure or gain a backend transaction.
- Plant modifiers enforce one field and one surface modifier; the clearer candidates already reflect this exclusivity.
- Painter, interaction, refresh, reload, status, metrics, and hardware-stat surfaces have backend homes. “Destination only” tools can be connected without inventing a new paradigm.
- Scene revisions are carried, but the HTTP boundary does not demonstrate compare-and-swap enforcement or a complete requested → accepted → applied → observed receipt chain. Drift/reapply UIs must not manufacture that certainty.
- Journeys, timed phase execution, and scheduling are absent and require a new service contract rather than a thin adapter.

## Cross-candidate kit of parts

Regardless of the winning paradigm, the production interface should combine these patterns:

1. **One authoritative physical-live strip** from Wall Conductor/Finder, visible in every workspace and at phone width. It must show power, running/stopped, full preset/scene name, provider, role, saved/drift state, and observation time.
2. **Separate Stop output and Power Off** from Finder, with Wall Conductor's explicit “output black; power remains on” receipt and Finder's reviewed power consequences.
3. **Room Tune's conventional Current → Proposed confirmation**, always containing full provider-qualified identities, current power/output state, exact global diff, command scope, and a post-command identity-bearing receipt.
4. **Lumen Loom's rehearsal and saved-not-live threshold**, with an ordinary keyboard-accessible confirm in addition to hold. Reapply must show base/current/proposed revisions and the exact drift.
5. **Wall Conductor's provider/role/readiness catalog plus Room Tune's provenance sentence.** Previewability must never imply executability. Build-only/quarantined commands need redundant UI and backend gates.
6. **Lumen Path's purpose filters and three-item comparison** for discovery, without making journeys the operator's only route to a direct preset or scene.
7. **Room Tune's expected-degraded explanation plus Wall Conductor's exact lane map.** Report transport sent, playback agreement, release acceptance, and physical verification separately. Never summarize stale consensus as health.
8. **An evidence lifecycle:** fixture/local/hardware source, last-observed time, Refresh requested/accepted with ID, then a separately timestamped observed result. Health must be consistent with power and output state.
9. **A real maintainer hub** linking provider collisions, readiness gates, receiver evidence, reload, painter, semantic foliage/globe masks, calibration evidence, developer/test content, and command receipts. Every control must say whether it is navigation-only, preview-only, or hardware-affecting.
10. **A single state subscription and command-risk rule:** opening a confirmation re-reads current state; submitting compares its revision; any mismatch blocks and offers Reload/Rebase while preserving the draft. A confirmation gesture is not evidence of application.

## Release-blocking acceptance tests

- Stop output, confirm power remains on, then Power Off; every live, scene, journey, and health surface must update consistently.
- Change live content and verify that receiver evidence cannot report the old background as healthy agreement without a conspicuous stale warning.
- Trigger receiver refresh and verify the UI distinguishes 202 acceptance from a later observation, including request identity and timestamps.
- Attempt live execution from every Build-only, Quarantined, Unavailable, and Developer-only entry; no path may serialize a command.
- Open a confirmation, change the wall from another client, then submit; the command must block with a current/proposed diff and recoverable rebase.
- Save a scene and prove Vibe, plant material, brightness, FPS, speed, power, and live output were not saved or changed.
- At 390 px, identify current physical state, Stop within one tap, find a calming preset, inspect provider/readiness, and complete a keyboard/touch-accessible old→new review without hidden or overlapping controls.
